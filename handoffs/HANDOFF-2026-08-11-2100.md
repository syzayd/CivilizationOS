## Decisions made, and why

- **Kept fig3/fig5 from the second build, fig4/fig6 from the original** - not a coin flip. Fig
  6's Wilson CI is the statistically correct choice for `results_decision.json`'s stored
  `(mean, std, n)` with no per-scenario array (the second build's normal-approximation CI
  under-covers near the boundary where `tcmf_shipped` sits at 0.97). Fig 4's tuned-lambda point
  needed the actual N03 held-out split, which only the original build ran.
- **F9b's claim is scoped to the small pool, not deleted.** `tcmf_add` still converges to the
  semantic floor at the small pool (Table tab:dropout, unchanged); at the realistic pool it
  degrades below the floor because at 100% dropout the score reduces to ranking by the episodic
  score, not the semantic-similarity score `semantic_rag` uses - the two never had to coincide.
- **N13's "ordering not preserved" is reported as a finding, not smoothed over.** The item's own
  verify criterion said to do this if it happened. The core operator contrast (`tcmf_mult` vs
  `tcmf_add`) survives; only the top-two-place swap between `causal_only`/`graph_ppr` does not.
- **N12's fix4 dose-response uses `max_mem_per_citizen` in {8,10,12,16,20,32}, realistic pool.**
  The benchmark's own `materialize()` keeps every citizen at or below `max_mem_per_citizen` by
  construction, so the old top-8 prune cap only bites once a citizen genuinely exceeds it - this
  sweep shows the effect is a smooth function of the excess, not one cherry-picked value.
